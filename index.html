Kōnane

Hawaiian checkers. Jump to capture, and the last player able to move wins.

Opponent
You play
Strength

Kōnane is traditionally played on a stone board with black basalt and white coral pebbles.

How the game works
  • Black and white stones fill the board in a checkerboard pattern.
  • To open, Black removes one of its own stones from a corner or the center. White then removes a stone directly beside that gap.
  • Black moves first after that. Every move is a capture: jump one of your stones over an adjacent enemy stone, in a straight line, into the empty square just beyond it. Diagonal jumps are not allowed.
  • The jumped stone is removed. You may keep jumping in the same direction in the same turn, taking several stones, and you may stop after any jump. You cannot change direction mid-turn.
  • If you have no capture on your turn, you lose.

Board sizes and small details differ from place to place. This follows the rules most commonly taught.
